## Final Project: Milestone 1

The final project is a substantial, open-ended assignment in which you select, implement, and analyze a parallel computing application using both **CUDA** and **MPI**.

One example option would be to build and optimize a small large-language model (LLM) from scratch, but you are free to propose any project that makes meaningful use of GPU acceleration and distributed-memory parallelism.

The project runs across the quarter through four milestones and culminates in a 6-page final report with accompanying code, due **Monday, June 8 at 11:59 PM**.

## Final Project Requirements

Every project **must** include the elements marked as required. The remaining elements are optional but may strengthen your report:

1. **CUDA implementation (required).** At least one computationally intensive kernel must be written in CUDA. You should demonstrate understanding of the GPU memory hierarchy (global, shared, registers), thread organization, and coalesced memory access.

2. **MPI implementation (required).** The application must run across multiple GPUs using MPI. Demonstrate correct use of point-to-point and/or collective communication.

3. **OpenMP implementation.** You may use OpenMP to parallelize CPU computations or to manage hybrid MPI/CUDA workflows from the host.

4. **Performance profiling and benchmarking (required).** Use profiling tools to measure kernel performance. Include scaling results (strong and/or weak scaling).

5. **Bottleneck and architecture analysis.** Discuss what limits your application's performance: is it compute-bound, memory-bound, communication-bound, or latency-bound?

6. **Theoretical performance analysis.** Develop a performance model for your application. This may include arithmetic intensity calculations, a Roofline analysis, and/or an isoefficiency analysis. Compare your measured performance against your theoretical bounds.

7. **Algorithmic optimizations (required).** Identify and implement at least one non-trivial optimization (e.g., tiling, memory coalescing, overlapping communication with computation, mixed precision). Quantify the improvement.

8. **Discussion and context (required).** Compare (quantitatively or just qualitatively) your approach and results with existing implementations or state-of-the-art methods. Discuss trade-offs, limitations, and potential future improvements.

## Milestone 1 Instructions

For Milestone 1, you will submit a proposal that outlines your project at a high level. This is not a binding contract—your project may evolve as you learn more throughout the quarter—but it should demonstrate that you have thought carefully about what you want to build and how you plan to approach it. This will be a group submission on Gradescope. Make sure to include all the members of your group in the submission. You are allowed between 1 and 3 members per group.

Your proposal must address the following:

1. **Project focus.** What problem are you solving? What is the application domain (e.g., deep learning, scientific simulation, computational physics, graph algorithms)? Why is parallelism important for this problem?

2. **Core algorithms.** What algorithms do you expect to use?

3. **Parallel computing approach.** How do you plan to use CUDA and MPI?

4. **Planned deliverables.** What do you hope to produce by the end of the quarter?

5. **Timeline.** Provide a plan for how you will meet each milestone. Identify any risks you foresee.

**Format:** The proposal must fit on **two pages** (single-spaced, 11pt font, 1-inch margins). You may use bullet points. Be concise and as specific as possible, although we realize this is the beginning of the quarter so you may not have all the answers yet.
